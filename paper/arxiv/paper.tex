\begin{abstract}
Modern machine learning research in ``world models'' has focused heavily on continuous control, video generation, and latent-space imagination for robotics and games. Concurrently, enterprise organizations demand simulation tools to answer prescriptive interventional questions: \emph{``What would happen if we enact decision $a$ instead of baseline $a'$?''}

When applied to organizational and socio-technical domains (such as supply chain resilience, capital allocation, and crisis logistics), unconstrained neural and generative world models suffer from catastrophic failure modes: they violate fundamental physical and accounting conservation laws, compound autoregressive rollout errors, and emit unjustified point forecasts.

In this paper, we introduce the \textbf{Enterprise World Model Engine (EWM)}, an open-source, domain-neutral structural simulation kernel. EWM establishes an architectural firewall between \textbf{KNOWN explicit conservation laws} (enforced as hard algebraic invariants) and \textbf{LEARNED probabilistic dynamics} (restricted to conservative nullspace projections). We establish the theoretical license for this architecture via Richens \& Everitt's Causal World Model Theorem (ICLR 2024), showing that robust interventional planning requires structural causal models (SCMs). EWM operationalizes this via: (1) a native SCM kernel supporting Pearl's three-step counterfactual algorithm under Twin-Rollout exogenous noise coupling; (2) amortized Simulation-Based Inference (SBI) with certified coverage evaluated through the Test of Accuracy of Related Probabilities (TARP); and (3) a beyond-rollout-error evaluation suite scoring models on \textbf{epistemic honesty}. Across six empirical benchmarks, EWM achieves bit-for-bit determinism, zero mass-balance drift ($0.000000$), and certified coverage calibration without mandatory GPU or LLM dependencies.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
In recent years, the paradigm of world models \cite{hafner2023dreamerv3} has emerged as a promising frontier in artificial intelligence. However, applying unconstrained generative world models to enterprise decision systems reveals acute hazards: physical quantities are hallucinated, point forecasts mask solvency tail risks, and observational associations collapse under policy shifts \cite{pearl2009causality}. We propose \textbf{Structural World Models (SWM)} to address these limitations.
